This often means a secondary controller, but some products use channel 9 for Linux application packages or other host-processor firmware. The reported channel alone does not identify the physical chip or CPU architecture.

The product key identifies the Tuya product for which the OTA was offered. The listed version is the target OTA version; a current version used to reveal an update is not evidence that the version ever shipped in a retail device.

The catalogue currently contains 264 unique OTA payloads across 268 product/version/URL offering records. Release-note text for 261 entries comes directly from cached Tuya channel-9 JSON; 3 superseded entries use text from their matching Tuya update screenshots; and 4 responses had no description. Original Tuya CDN URLs were captured for 261 payloads and may stop working independently of the archived files.

Filenames include the product identification and, where supported by direct evidence, an MCU identification. Changelog wording is retained from Tuya. Matching update screenshots, where available, are in [`images/`](images/).

A single payload may be offered for multiple product keys. Its filename reflects one confirmed identification; each row retains the individual offering's key, version, release notes and URL.
